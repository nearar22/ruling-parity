# Originality and authority note

RulingParity compares the application of one rule across two decisions. Unlike source verification, it does not decide whether a claim has evidence; unlike document diffing, it does not ask whether two texts preserve meaning; unlike proposal mapping, it does not seek common ground. Its material state change is a finalized consistency receipt derived from rule-relevant fact similarity and normalized dispositions.

The contract treats the three caller-selected URLs as evidence, not automatically trusted authorities. Production users must choose an official rule publisher and authentic decision repositories. The bundled records are explicitly synthetic fixtures.
